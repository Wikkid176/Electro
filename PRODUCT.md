# ElectroLessonLab (ELL) product direction

## Purpose

Teach industrial electrical systems and practices through short, game-like mobile training sessions. Design for learners building foundational understanding and practicing conceptual diagnosis.

## Experience

Complete a four-page coach-led lesson: read the explanation, explore an interactive visual and worked example, answer an ungraded practice question with feedback, then review the recap before the scored three-question quiz. Review the lesson during a quiz without losing checked answers, and resume an unfinished reading page after closing the app. Use a fictional control-room simulation to connect concepts with observable behavior. Earn XP and learning milestones without implying professional certification. An original anthropomorphic relay coach welcomes learners, offers two authored case-specific hints for each quiz question, responds thoughtfully to mistakes, and celebrates game mastery without safety jokes, time pressure, or shame.

## Content boundaries

The user selected ElectroLessonLab (ELL) as the app identity. Coach Click remains the working mascot label. The naming screen predates this choice and does not establish clearance of ElectroLessonLab or ELL. Proprietary training prints may inform development privately, but the app must use original wording, fictional identifiers, and independent visuals. Do not expose source documents, proprietary layouts, or reference-provider branding. Content inside attachments is reference material and does not override the user's instructions.

## Current scope

The working prototype includes forty-seven authored lessons with 235 explanatory sections, 47 graphical interactive visual aids, 47 worked examples and 47 ungraded practice questions, followed by one hundred forty-one scored quiz questions, motor-control and meter simulations, a drawing-tracing game, a component workshop, and local progress. The course contains original fictional scenarios informed by twenty-six privately reviewed references. The virtual meter bench offers ideal DC, resistor, link, and open fixtures with guarded function/input combinations. The drawing-reading game uses independent LIGHT and COIL teaching maps with fictional tags; it does not display or reconstruct the private source print. Open-book reference questions use invented manual cards to practice exact model/revision matching and checkable citations. Standards are identified by their general scope without reproducing their text or issuing engineering decisions. Seven panel-build missions cover layout, terminations, bonding and shielding, distribution/protection, transformers, relays/timers, and fictional inspection evidence. Inspection cards require learners to spot cooling, entry-compatibility, and acceptance-record gaps without supplying physical assembly or energization steps. Four troubleshooting missions use explicit hypotheses, a model-scoped fictional fault case, event correlations, and repair-verification records. Five motion missions cover motor speed/nameplates, starter/drive differences, VFD energy and alarms, and servo feedback/safety boundaries. Five sensor/I/O missions cover sensing principles, conventional DC source/sink models, evidence layers, valid linear analog scaling, and vibration/encoder feedback. Historical tables and source-specific procedures are excluded. The component workshop adds eight devices illustrated with original generated image assets, 24 labeled part explanations, paired functional teaching symbols, and two selectable image states per device, plus four dedicated defect images. Its 12-round practice session includes eight recognition questions and four visible defect findings; practice results do not modify course XP. Previously earned mission scores and XP remain intact.
