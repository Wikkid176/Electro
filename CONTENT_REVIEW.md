# Curriculum review

## Material reviewed

Text was extracted and reviewed from the individually supplied Module 1 safety/LOTO study guide (revision dated October 2026), electrical safety presentation (2009), and hazardous-energy-control procedure (2016). Source documents and extracted text remain outside this checkout. No source illustrations or proprietary prints were included in app assets.

The sources informed topic selection, not reusable field instructions. All new teaching text, questions, answer choices, and explanations were independently written for fictional scenarios. Source-provider branding, machine numbers, device tags, sheet references, wire-color conventions, workplace locations, and source-specific procedures are excluded.

## Source issues deliberately excluded

- The 2009 slides use historical category tables, PPE language, distances, and numerical claims. They are background material rather than a current ruleset. No tables, injury photographs, or marketing examples were reused.
- The 2016 procedure contains site-specific servicing exceptions and a no-start verification description. These were not adopted as general permission to skip lockout or as an electrical absence-of-voltage method.
- The newer study guide identifies gaps in the older procedure and includes machine-specific electrical examples. Its date does not independently establish technical correctness or applicability. Exact test connections, meter modes, equipment wait times, PPE selections, and sample panel labels were not turned into field instructions.
- Conflicting documents must be resolved by the responsible safety authority using applicable requirements and approved procedures. The app does not tell a learner to choose a rule solely because it appears stricter.

## Release review still required

A qualified electrical-safety reviewer must verify the curriculum against current applicable regulations, the adopted standards edition, equipment documentation, and the employer's approved program. In particular, review qualification versus authorization, electrical verification, group and shift-transfer protection, exceptional lock removal, temporary re-energization, and the treatment of shock/arc-flash controls.

An attempt to retrieve the official OSHA energy-control regulation from this cloud machine was blocked by the network proxy (HTTP 403). No independent current-standards verification or compliance certification is claimed. The app deliberately omits numerical boundaries, voltage thresholds, PPE ratings, and regulatory exception checklists rather than infer them from historical sources.

App tests check gameplay, data preservation, and rendering; they do not establish electrical correctness, work authorization, or regulatory compliance. This remains conceptual training pending qualified content review and native-device validation.

### Source limitations addressed

- An open circuit was not described as making the entire installation de-energized. Watts and volt-amperes were not treated as interchangeable for all AC loads. Ideal resistor calculations are explicitly scoped.
- Residential supply assumptions, jurisdiction-specific conductor colors, and descriptions of neutral current returning through earth were not imported into the industrial lessons.
- Broad claims about digital-meter accuracy, analogue-meter placement, or universal meter-category/voltage suitability were not adopted. Suitability depends on the complete instrument setup and application.
- A continuity tone is not proof of component health or safety; a lack of tone is not necessarily an open path. Thresholds and range behavior are instrument-specific.
- A low-impedance reading collapsing does not independently establish a safe circuit. No field instructions for changing impedance or probing energized equipment are provided.
- Insulation-test voltages, pass/fail limits, discharge times, live-testing instructions, and physical rescue techniques were excluded. These require equipment-specific procedures and qualified review. Non-contact indication is not presented as a substitute for the required electrical verification process.
- True-RMS and event-recording capabilities are presented with their waveform, bandwidth, and capture limitations.

### Virtual meter assumptions

The bench is an independently coded, fictional two-terminal model with an ideal 18 V DC source, an isolated 220 Ω resistor, an isolated 0.2 Ω link, and an isolated open path. Isolated fixtures are defined to have no external/stored energy or parallel paths; selecting one does not simulate or teach the process of making real equipment safe.

Only DC voltage, resistance, and continuity are modeled. The A-input choice illustrates an incompatible input and is always blocked; no direct-current measurement operation is simulated. Resistance and continuity on the energized source are blocked. Reversing the virtual reference changes only DC polarity. The example continuity threshold is below 30 Ω and is not a universal instrument specification. A tone is displayed visually, without audio. Zero voltage, OL, and continuity displays never authorize field work.

The existing safety-review requirements still apply. Before release, qualified reviewers should additionally verify the calculations, meter terminology, complete measurement-system rating language, interpretation limitations, and the boundaries of the simulation against current equipment manuals and applicable requirements.

### Fictional tracing game

The app’s independent TRAIN-LAMP teaching package uses two vertical symbol-tile maps, LIGHT and COIL, with A/B location labels and DEMO device tags. These are educational representations, not compliant installation schematics. They deliberately do not share the source print’s identifiers, sheet organization, appearance, or machine functions.

Five targets take the learner from an indicator to its preceding relay contact, to that contact’s associated coil on the other sheet, to the command switch, and back to the load return. A device reference is explicitly labeled as a relationship rather than a conductor connection. Highlighting records successful selections; it does not represent energization, actual contact position, or safety status. Incorrect selections do not advance the exercise. Completion and reset affect only this practice session and do not award or erase course XP.

Qualified review should verify the original exercise’s notation, reference relationships, normal-state explanations, and return-versus-protective-earth language before a training release. This app is not a digital twin of the referenced machine and must not be used to identify real wiring or plan changes to that equipment.

### Simplified source claims not adopted

Troubleshooting sequences from the workplace guide were not copied as operating or testing procedures. Its live half-splitting examples, source-specific circuit paths, fault-location claims, supply tolerances, safety-relay light/reset interpretations, power-cycle advice, branch-disconnection sequences, and overload-setting shortcuts were excluded. A reading at a load does not by itself prove the complete electrical path or a failed load, and a voltage drop is not universally a simple resistance diagnosis. Return paths, alternate feeds, loading, waveform, reference, command state, and instrumentation can change the inference. No live wiggle test, output force, safety bypass, repeated reset, or physical probing method appears in the exercises.

SIM-PATH and CASE-MODEL/CASE-RECORD are independent invented teaching records. The exercise explicitly states one intended series path, one open, a known source/reference, intact return, no parallel or alternate paths, and a common snapshot. Its B–C inference is confined to those assumptions; it does not diagnose a real machine, prescribe measurements, identify a particular failed component, or establish isolation. No generic logarithmic measurement-count guarantee is made.

The motion sources' motor lead diagrams, specific machine/servo topology, part numbers, network/safety configurations, fault/parameter tables, discharge times, DC-link multipliers, test connections, insulation-test voltages and acceptance thresholds, polarization-index bands, temperature shortcuts, current-unbalance ratios, service-factor allowances, and motor-protection settings were not reused. A general volts-per-hertz statement was not converted into guaranteed torque at every speed. The app does not prescribe reversing leads, motor connections, insulation testing, tuning, braking-resistor selection, ramp changes, bus verification, or a commissioning sequence.

The shielding paper discusses application-specific methods, including intermediate shield arrangements, that differ from the study guide's simplified rules. Neither source becomes a universal shield recipe. Exact drive/cable/system instructions and responsible design review govern actual choices. No source termination drawing, pigtail dimensions, clamp assembly, or installation instruction was imported. STO is not presented as electrical isolation, capacitor discharge, load restraint, or a guaranteed stop. A holding brake is not presented as adequate servicing protection beneath a suspended load.

The sensor source's M12 pin/color tables, connector ratings, machine signal topology, specific excitation/bias ranges, fixed sensor-distance factors, NAMUR failure-band summaries, loop-breaking/injection methods, and blanket one-end shield rule were excluded. Sensor LEDs do not have a universal meaning or presence; a disagreement between LED and PLC data does not uniquely prove a cable fault. PNP/NPN pairings are explicitly scoped to a conventional DC model and do not replace exact diagrams. Zero loop current and unusual IEPE bias are diagnostic clues rather than unique cause determinations. Absolute encoders are not assumed to retain every multi-turn state through every power-loss condition.

All numerical examples were independently selected: a four-pole 40 Hz motor model, a 120-cycle/revolution encoder at 300 RPM, and a −20 to 60 °C valid linear 4–20 mA channel. A/B edge decoding is separated from channel cycle frequency. The model-scoped analog formula does not convert invalid current into a trusted process value or provide a calibration procedure.

Qualified reviewers must validate diagnostic reasoning and its assumptions, motor/drive and safety-function descriptions, source/sink terminology, signal validity, scaling, feedback counts, and the limits of these examples. Software tests exercise rendering, scoring, and progress preservation; electrical correctness and field authorization remain outside their scope.
