## Recommended licensing direction

For a paid course with independently owned curriculum and branding, a possible approach is commercial terms for genuinely owned future contributions/content, with explicit third-party and prior-license exceptions. This is a recommendation to evaluate, not an applied change. **The current published MIT scope must be reviewed first.** Commercial terms cannot revoke valid MIT grants already made, claim ownership of an employer's work, or create exclusive copyright in purely generated material. Keeping the code MIT and charging for supported distribution/new services is also a legitimate commercial model.

## What requires your input

1. Identify the legal person/entity claiming ELL and whether work was done within employment, on employer time/resources, under an invention-assignment agreement, or with employer/customer confidential information. Fill this privately using the owner statement.
2. Confirm the origin and rights holder for each relevant source item. Uploading or possessing a document is not commercial permission. An employer cannot grant a publisher's or customer's rights that it does not own.
3. Choose the intended future licensing model. No new proprietary or MIT license for your original work is applied by this packet.
4. Where needed, personalize the request and obtain an authorized written response. A source-use permission and a release of claims to the app are separate issues; request both if applicable.
5. Have counsel review the actual agreements, historical license scope and signed permissions. Only then finalize owner identity, scope and effective date of operative terms.

**Keep completed documents private.** Do not commit employment agreements, signatures, addresses, customer drawings or filled permission schedules to this public repository. The published files are blank reusable templates. Send completed materials to the intended employer/rights holder/lawyer through an agreed private channel. No message has been sent on your behalf.
