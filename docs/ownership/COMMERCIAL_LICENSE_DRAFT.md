## Terms to have counsel finalize

1. **End-user grant.** Define personal learner use and employer named-seat use, device/reinstall allowances and access to the purchased course version. Do not sell ownership of third-party software or imply a user loses its open-source rights.
2. **Updates/support.** Specify included fixes/support and separately priced future products, if any. Do not promise perpetual updates, certification, LMS/SSO or reports that are not provided.
3. **Third-party rights.** Provide the actual third-party license/NOTICE materials and any source/access information required. Nothing in commercial terms should restrict rights those licenses independently provide.
4. **Content/brand rights.** Define any restrictions only to the extent supported by actual rights and law. Do not assert ownership of electrical principles, standards names or third-party publications. Address mark use separately.
5. **Purchases/refunds.** Align price, store terms, withdrawal/refund rights, seat assignment and entitlement restoration with the implemented transaction model and applicable law.
6. **Training limits and accuracy.** Describe conceptual training and the limits of quiz achievements without implying an official credential. A disclaimer is not a substitute for accurate material, appropriate review or nonwaivable legal duties.
7. **Data and employer use.** Match the actual local/manual sharing system and any future backend. Keep privacy, retention and employee access obligations explicit.
8. **Enforcement and governing terms.** Use appropriate US/state-specific terms and lawful limitations. Do not assume all reverse-engineering, statutory, consumer or open-source rights can be waived.

## Open-source alternative

If the owner chooses MIT for the original code, retain the applicable notices and explicitly document which original work is actually licensed under MIT by the authorized owner. Charging for distribution, reviewed updates, support or employer services can coexist with MIT. Curriculum, trademarks and artwork need their own deliberate scope; do not claim that an open-source choice automatically creates exclusive rights in them.

## Application checklist

Do not apply operative terms until: owner authority is documented; employment/source issues are resolved for the intended use; prior grants are accounted for; commercial/open-source choice is confirmed; component/source obligations are closed; and counsel has reviewed the final scope and terms. This draft is delivered for review, not for use as a completed agreement.
